Milestone 4 - Prototype, Testing & Evaluation
It is now finally time to start coding the first prototype of your proposed software. You will then test and evaluate the produced prototype relative to the Requirements and Design you have created in the previous milestones.

In Weeks 7, 8, 9 and 10, the lectures will discuss Implementation, Testing, Code Management and DevOps. You will then apply this knowledge in this milestone.

Milestone Submission & Assessment
The final milestone for the project is building your Prototype, Testing & Evaluation. It is worth 50% of the overall module grade.

This milestone is assessed based on the graded group submission; however, in order to be given a grade each team member must also complete an individual evidence submission.

Feedback and marks will be provided after Easter, alongside the feedback and marks for Milestone 3.

 

Your Individual & Group Task
First Prototype [25 marks]
As a team, you are to develop your proposed software (Milestone 1), based on the agreed-upon Design (Milestone 3), which itself was based upon the Requirements (Milestone 2) that you decided on as a group. Note that your previous Milestones are not binding: changes made along the way are perfectly acceptable (but must be explicitly justified in your Evaluation, including the impact on scope and feasibility). Remember that you are only developing the first prototype of your proposed software -- we do not expect a full system to be created, but the result of your first software 'sprint'.

Each group has been provided with a GitLab repository, which you must use to evidence your progress. You should commit every time you work on your project, and must not work on your own device or in a different repository, only committing everything towards the end of the project. A pattern of commits all occurring shortly before the deadline may negatively affect the assessment of contribution.

A basic introduction to using GitLab is available here: Getting Started with GitLabLinks to an external site.

Your GitLab repository's README file must include instructions on how to download and run your project, as well as any libraries or requirements we will require to run it. You must ensure that we can run your project: any project that cannot be run will be significantly penalised.

Individual Statement [Evidence: Required to gain a mark]
Part 1: Contributions Statement
It is important that each member of your group makes a meaningful contribution in terms of implementing a defined feature of your prototype, either on their own or in a sub-team. This could be based on your suggested split of work in Milestone 3, though this is not binding.

Therefore, each member of the team must write a short statement explaining their contribution to the project, which will be further evidenced by their individual Git activity. A team member's contribution cannot be wholly restricted to Testing and/or Evaluation: each group member must be responsible for implementing a clearly identifiable feature or component of the prototype.

Part 2: Generative AI Statement
Your individual statement must also declare any substantive use of Generative AI by that individual group member.

You are permitted to use Generative AI as a tool to during Milestone 4, though you are not required to do so. The purpose of this module is to focus on the process of Software Engineering; therefore overly relying on Generative AI will not enable you to do this. Furthermore, a lack of evidence of the development process may affect your overall grade: if you generate your prototype wholesale with Generative AI, you will not be able to sufficiently evaluate your process through your poster.

If you do choose to use AI to generate material, you must clearly describe how the material was generated (including the prompts used), what output was produced, and how that output was subsequently modified by you. Include relevant excerpts of generated output where appropriate.

IMPORTANT: Failure to declare substantive use of Generative AI, or failure to appropriately reference externally sourced, non-original work, may constitute academic misconduct under the University’s Code of Practice on Academic IntegrityLinks to an external site..

You should use the following structure:

The following prompts were input into [AI system]: <List prompt(s)>
The output obtained was: <Relevant excerpts or summary of output>
The output was changed by me in the following ways: <Explain modifications and how you exercised independent judgement>
Unit & Integration Testing [10 marks]
You should develop a Test Plan for your prototype that includes both Unit Testing and Integration Testing:

Unit Testing must be performed against the individual features of your prototype,
Integration Testing must be performed after your whole prototype has been developed.
You must provide the evidence of your Test Plan, and your completed Unit & Integration Testing, in your GitLab repository.

Reflective Evaluation [15 marks]
After your prototype and testing has been completed, the final step is to evaluate your project, reflecting on:

The problem you set out to solve in Milestone 1 (How well does your prototype start to solve this problem? Did you make any changes along the way? Discuss your successes, as well as any unexpected difficulties and what you would need to focus on in a second prototype.),
The Requirements you decided upon in Milestone 2 (Which have you achived? Which have you not achieved, and why? Did you make any changes along the way? Discuss what you would need to focus on in a second prototype),
The Design Plan you decided upon in Milestone 3 (Were the features you focused on the right ones? Were the technologies you decided upon appropriate? Did you make any changes along the way? Discuss anything you would have done differently, or any adjustments you would make for a second prototype.)
Your individual software development process, and your process as a team, throughout the development of Milestone 4 (How did your team co-ordinate its work? What was your approach to developing your individual aspect of the prototype? How did your individual processes feed into the groupwork? What were the significant challenges faced along the way?)
Your Evaluation must be illustrated in an A1 academic poster to be (optionally) presented on the Software Showcase Day. The poster should also include a brief introduction to your project, and how it addresses some aspect of SDG 11. Marks are awarded for the quality of evaluation and reflection, not graphic design, although please see the below for suitable design advice.
